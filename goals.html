Career goals

Put AI on the hardware that needs it most.

Long term, I want to be a systems engineer working on AI infrastructure at the edge: the chips, firmware, and runtimes that let models run on satellites, phones, and sensors instead of only in data centers. Today most AI lives in a cloud region, and data has to travel to it. For a satellite or a field sensor, that trip is slow, expensive, or impossible. I want to help flip that around so compute moves to the data.

The company I'd most like to start my career at is AWS Annapurna Labs, which designs the Nitro, Graviton, and Trainium silicon behind AWS. It sits right where hardware and large-scale software meet. After a few years building and shipping at that layer, I'd like to start or join an early-stage company working on onboard compute for space or edge devices.

How I'll get there

1. Build the fundamentals (2026–2027)

I'm taking ECE 2035 now, writing C and RISC-V assembly. Next I need computer architecture and operating systems, which are the two areas I've learned mostly on my own. In Spring 2027 I plan to take machine learning so I understand the models I want to deploy, not just the systems around them.

2. Choose threads that match the goal

I'm moving my threads to Distributed System & Software Design and Systems & Architecture. Together they cover how a single chip runs code and how many machines work together, which is exactly the gap in edge AI.

3. Keep doing real research

At ANSR I'm working on virtualizing satellite resources, and at NEXS I'm studying how LLM serving systems behave under agent workloads. Those two projects are the two halves of my goal. I also want to apply for a President's Undergraduate Research Award to fund a deeper project.

4. Get industry experience at the hardware/software boundary

My target for Summer 2027 is a systems or embedded internship. I'm applying broadly to big tech, aerospace, and startups, and I'm building embedded projects so my resume shows I can work below the operating system, not just above it.

5. Keep shipping

Every semester I want at least one project that's public, documented, and used by someone. Projects like Mesh Search and my Discovery Project are how I prove the coursework stuck.
